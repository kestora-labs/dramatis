# Critical commentary on *The Salt Road*

**Author:** synthetic — written for this repository, standing in for third-party scholarship.
**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, distinct from the work itself.

This document is neither the narrative nor the author's own reference material. It is a
third party asserting relationships about the work, and Dramatis must be able to attribute
its claims separately and exclude them on export.

## On the renaming of the confidante

The change from Hesper (1889) to Perdita (1903) is the most discussed alteration in the
revised edition. The function of the character is unaltered — she is the one who holds
Corin to a commitment he has already begun to abandon — and no line of hers differs beyond
the name.

Editors disagree on whether this constitutes one character or two. This corpus takes the
position that it is one character with an edition-scoped surface form, and requires that any
graph be able to state which edition a claim came from.

## On Corin and Marlow

The antagonism is legal rather than personal, and the 1903 revision sharpens this: Hesper's
appeal in 1889 rests on forty families, while Perdita's in 1903 rests on forty families *and
on prescriptive right*. The relationship between Corin and Marlow is unchanged; what changes
is the ground of the argument.

## On the absence of a fourth figure

The magistrate is named in both editions and appears in neither. A relationship graph that
places him on the page has confused mention with presence — a distinction this corpus exists
partly to test.
