# Chapter Three

Auber found him at the bridge, which was the one place he had promised himself he would
not go.

"You're Kell."

The inspector turned. He was younger than Auber had built him. "I am."

"Forty years I've walked that round."

"I know," Kell said. "It's in the file. Forty-one, actually, if the first winter counts."

"It counts."

"Then forty-one." Kell closed his notebook, which Auber had not expected. "Mr Vance, the
bridge end is going to gas whether I write it or someone else does. I'd rather it were me,
because I'll argue for the half you keep and another man won't."

"You want thanking."

"No," Kell said. "I want you to stop coming to the bridge. It doesn't help you and it makes
it harder for me to argue."

Auber stood there a long moment with nothing to hold. Then he went home the long way, past
ninety-one lamps he no longer had the right to light, and did not tell Neve where he had
been.
