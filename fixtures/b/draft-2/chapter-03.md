# Chapter Three

Neve found Kell at the bridge, which was where the notices said he would be, and where her
father had promised himself he would not go.

"You're Kell."

The inspector turned. He was younger than she had built him. "I am."

"My father walked that round forty years."

"I know," Kell said. "It's in the file. Forty-one, actually, if the first winter counts."

"It counts."

"Then forty-one." Kell closed his notebook, which she had not expected. "Miss Vance, the
bridge end is going to gas whether I write it or someone else does. I'd rather it were me,
because I'll argue for the half he keeps and another man won't."

"You want thanking."

"No," Kell said. "I want him to stop coming to the bridge. It doesn't help him and it makes
it harder for me to argue."

Neve went home the long way, past ninety-one lamps her father no longer had the right to
light, and worked out what she would tell him and what she would not.

She told him the half was safe. She did not tell him she had asked.
