# The Salt Road — First Edition (1889)

## I

Corin Ashe came down to the salt road in the last week of the season, when the carts had
gone and the ruts had set hard, and found Hesper waiting where she had said she would wait.

"You came," she said.

"I said I would."

"People say a great deal." Hesper fell into step beside him. "Did you speak to Marlow?"

"I spoke to him."

"And?"

Corin was a long time answering. "He says the road is his to close."

"It isn't."

"He says it is, and he has the paper, and the paper is what a magistrate reads." He kicked a
ridge of set mud. "Hesper, I don't think this ends the way you want it to."

"It doesn't have to end the way I want it to," she said. "It has to end with the road open."

## II

Marlow received them in the counting-house, standing, which he did when he intended not to
be argued with.

"Miss Hesper," he said, and to Corin, nothing at all.

"You know why we've come."

"I know why you think you've come." Marlow set his hands on the desk. "The road crosses my
ground. That is all the fact there is in the matter."

"Forty families use it."

"Then forty families have been fortunate for a long while," Marlow said, "and are about to
be less so."
