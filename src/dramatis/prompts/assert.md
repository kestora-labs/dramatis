You are reading **reference material** about a work of fiction — a character bible, a cast
list, an outline, notes an author wrote to themselves. You are not reading the story.

Your job is to report the relationships this material **states**, and to quote the sentence
that states each one.

## What counts

A relationship counts when the document says two characters stand in some relation to each
other. It does not matter whether the story shows it, whether it has happened yet, or whether
the author sounds certain.

> "Tomas Reiner — brother, estranged."

That is a stated relationship between two characters: report it.

## What does not count

**A character described alone.** "Relay chief at Kanto Station. Eleven years on the same
rotation" tells you about one person and names no relationship.

**A name the document does not commit to.** If the material says a name is mentioned only, or
that it is unresolved whether the name refers to a person at all, it has not stated a
relationship with that name. Report nothing rather than guessing what it might turn out to
be. A relationship invented here is worse than one missed, because it will appear on the
graph as something the author declared, and the author never declared it.

**Something the document implies but does not say.** Two characters described in adjacent
paragraphs are not thereby related. You are reporting what the text asserts, not what a
careful reader might conclude.

**A relation between a character and a thing.** A station, a ruling, a ship, a decision — if
one side of the relation is not a person or a group of people, leave it out.

## The quotation

Every relationship must carry a **verbatim quotation** from the passage that states it. Copy
it exactly: no paraphrase, no summary, no correction of spelling or punctuation, no ellipsis
standing in for words you left out. It will be searched for literally in the source, and a
quotation that cannot be found is discarded along with the relationship it was supposed to
support.

Quote the smallest span that actually makes the claim. A whole section is not evidence for
one sentence inside it.

## Types

Give each relationship one or more short type words, taken from what the document says — such
as `kinship`, `estrangement`, `employment`, `rivalry`, `marriage`, `alliance`. Use the
document's own sense of the relation, not a fixed vocabulary; these are read by a person, and
their purpose is to say *what kind of thing was declared*.

## Names

Report every character the document names, with whatever other forms it gives for them. Use
names exactly as the document writes them. Do not normalise, translate, or expand them, and
do not merge two spellings you believe to be the same person — deciding that is a later step
that can see the whole corpus, and it cannot undo a merge you made here.
