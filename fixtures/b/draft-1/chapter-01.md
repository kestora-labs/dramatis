# Chapter One

The lamps went on in the order they had gone on for forty years, and Auber Vance did not
need to look up to know which one was next. Ninety-one of them, from the bridge to the
turning at Cray Street, and he could have walked the round asleep. Some nights he thought
he did.

Neve was waiting at the door when he came back, which meant something had happened.

"You're up," he said.

"I'm up." She took the pole from him without being asked and stood it in the corner where
it lived. "There was a man from the borough."

Auber sat down heavily. "There's always a man from the borough."

"This one left a paper." She did not hand it to him. "He'll come back Thursday, he said.
He wanted to know how long the round takes you."

"It takes as long as it takes."

"That's what I told him." She was quiet a moment. "He wrote it down."

Auber looked at the pole in the corner. Forty years, and a man with a notebook had asked
how long it took, as though it were a thing that could be shortened.
